How to actually redact a PDF (not just draw a black box)

Published 20 August 2026 · 5 min read

Every so often a redacted document makes the news for the wrong reason: someone highlights the black box, hits copy, and pastes the "hidden" text straight into a chat window. It keeps happening because most tools that call themselves redaction tools do something much simpler than redacting, they draw a black rectangle on top of the page. Visually, the text is gone. Underneath, in the file itself, it's exactly where it always was, selectable, searchable, and extractable by anyone who knows to try.

Why the black-box approach fails

A PDF page isn't a photograph, it's a set of instructions: draw this text at this position, in this font. Drawing a black rectangle just adds one more instruction on top, it doesn't delete the instructions underneath. Open the file in a text editor, run it through any PDF text-extraction tool, or in some viewers just select-and-copy the "covered" area, and the original text comes right back out. The visual cover and the underlying data are two completely separate things, and covering one does nothing to the other.

What actually works

The reliable fix is to stop thinking of the page as text-plus-a-box and turn the whole page into a picture instead, with the box already painted into it. A flattened image has no text layer to extract in the first place, there's nothing to select or search because it's just pixels. That's the approach Redact PDF takes: any page you mark gets rasterised, box included, before being placed back into the PDF. Pages you don't touch are left completely alone, so you're not paying the file-size or searchability cost on parts of the document that don't need it.

Step by step

  1. Open Redact PDF and add your file.
  2. Pick a page from the thumbnails.
  3. Click and drag on the preview to draw a box over anything sensitive, a name, an account number, a photo, whatever needs to go. Draw as many boxes as you need.
  4. Repeat on any other pages, a small red dot marks pages that have at least one box.
  5. Click "Redact & Download."

A few practical tips

  • Draw boxes a little larger than the text itself. A tight box that just clips the top or bottom of a character can leave enough of a shape for someone to guess at, err on the side of extra margin.
  • Redacted pages lose their text layer, on purpose. That page becomes an image, so its text won't be selectable or searchable afterward, that's the trade-off for the content actually being gone. Pages with no boxes drawn keep their original text intact.
  • Check the result before you send it. Open the downloaded PDF and try to select text where you redacted, nothing should highlight. That's the whole point.
  • This handles the file itself, not how you share it. If you're emailing the redacted PDF, that's a separate consideration, this tool guarantees the content is gone from the file you download.
